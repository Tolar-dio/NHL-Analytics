# Notes for Claude

This is Tolar's NHL analytics repository. The goal is league-wide analysis: team strength from xG and shot share, luck regression (shooting % and save % vs process), goaltender GSAx, deployment and line usage, special teams, and betting and fantasy hockey angles.

## Data

- Load the flat tables first: `data/tables/<season>/{games,events,shifts,players}.csv.gz` (pandas reads .gz directly).
- Raw API JSON lives in `data/raw/<season>/<game_id>/` if a field is missing from the tables.
- `scripts/fetch.py` downloads and rebuilds; `python scripts/fetch.py --tables-only` rebuilds tables from raw files without network access.
- The sandbox can usually reach api-web.nhle.com and api.nhle.com now (Oct 2026) with the repo's User-Agent, but the nightly GitHub Action is the source of truth for data; don't commit hand-fetched game data.
- `fetch.py` keeps re-downloading a recent game's shift charts until every skater's shift total matches his boxscore TOI (the feed fills in for hours after a game; six Oct 15, 2026 games were saved short). `analysis/validate_toi.py` checks season shift totals against the official stats-REST time-on-ice report in the nightly run.

## Conventions in events.csv

- `situation_code` is the NHL's 4-digit code: away goalie, away skaters, home skaters, home goalie. "1551" is 5v5; "1451" is a home power play 5v4; a 0 in a goalie slot means empty net.
- `x`, `y` are rink coordinates in feet from center ice. Which net a team shoots at flips by period; use `home_defending_side` to normalize before computing shot distance.
- For goals, player1 is the scorer and player2/player3 the assists; for blocked shots, player1 is the shooter, player2 the blocker, and `team` is the SHOOTING team (verified against boxscores); for faceoffs, player1 won.
- Shootout events have period 5 and period_type SO; exclude them from shot metrics.

## Working style

- Separate process from results. Flag small samples explicitly; single-game line stats are noise.
- Tag confidence: [Certain], [Likely], [Guessing].
- Any betting or wager tracking belongs in a separate private repository, not here (this one is public).

## Supplementary data

- MoneyPuck shots: `data/external/moneypuck/<season>/<game_id>.csv.gz` (their `xGoal` is the benchmark for our own xG model). Credit MoneyPuck.com in anything shown publicly.
- NHL Edge: `data/external/edge/<season>/skaters.csv.gz` (flattened) and `skaters_raw.json.gz` (full per-player JSON, includes per-area shot counts and record-game overlays).
- HTML time-on-ice reports (`toi_TH.htm.gz`, `toi_TV.htm.gz` in a game's raw folder) are the fallback when the shift JSON is empty; `fetch.py` parses them into shifts.csv. Overlapping JSON shifts are merged. Validated: every player-game TOI within 30s of the boxscore.

## Past seasons

- `data/raw/<season>/` and `data/tables/<season>/` hold each season separately; 20252026 is backfilled via `.github/workflows/backfill.yml`.
- `data/external/moneypuck_summary/<season>/` has MoneyPuck season summaries (skaters, goalies, teams, lines) by situation. Use last season as the prior for this season's shrinkage instead of league average; carry player priors with the player, not the team (rosters change).

## Analysis scripts

- `analysis/player_flags.py`: our own xG model (logistic, out-of-fold) plus skater and goalie tables. Run first.
- `analysis/onice.py`: 5v5 on-ice CF/xG/G and relative xGF% per skater.
- xG source: MoneyPuck's xGoal is primary (AUC 0.769 vs ours 0.752 on the same 2025-27 shots; aggregates correlate 0.95+). Ours fills gaps: `player_flags.py` trains on last season plus this one, out of fold grouped by game, with sequence features (time/distance/speed since the previous event, royal-road crossing, previous event type and team, shooter's shift time), which lifted it from 0.740. The same features did NOT help the live ESPN model (72 games: AUC 0.721 vs 0.722), so `xg_live.py` keeps its simpler set.
- On-ice attribution: a player is on ice for an event if start_sec < t <= end_sec.
- `analysis/props_data.py <season>` builds per-game tables into `analysis/output/<season>/`; run it for both seasons before modeling.
- `analysis/models.py`: anytime goal, 2+ points, and game totals, with last-season priors that follow the player. Modes: `season-backtest 20252026`, `prior-backtest`, `tonight` (reads `analysis/slate.csv`).
- Backtest results (2025-26, 1,241 games, 44k player-games, walk-forward): goal and 2+ point probabilities are well calibrated and beat a naive baseline every month (log loss goal 0.3924 vs 0.4008, 2+ 0.2653 vs 0.2792; 3+ predicted 877 vs 880 actual; 1+ runs about 3% high). Game totals do NOT beat league average in a useful way (corr 0.08, RMSE 2.284 vs 2.290): treat totals as context and compare them with the market line, never as a betting edge. `analysis/backtest_static.py` rebuilds the Trust page's `site/static/backtest_20252026.json` from a season backtest.
- Model settings (Oct 2026 walk-forward tests, `K` in `models.py`): goalie factors shrunk much harder (Kg 300, Kg0 600; goalie seasons barely repeat), on-ice finishing `Kof` 50 (relative to league, last season carried at `Kof_prior` 150), which fixed the underrating of stars (2+ points for 0.9+ point-a-game players: 25.7% predicted vs 27.9% actual before, 28.2% after), `PTS_CAL` 1.015, back-to-back factor `B2B` 0.025 (tired teams got 47.4% of xG in 2025-26). Tested and left off: finishing shrinkage in shot units (no change), negative-binomial points (over-predicts 3+), smaller Kx/Ko/Ki. In early 2026-27 (3k player-games, with priors) Kof scored slightly worse on log loss but still fixed star calibration; the live record decides.

## Dashboard

- `site/build.py` writes `site/data/*.json` (gitignored); the nightly workflow's `site` job runs the models, builds, and deploys `site/` to GitHub Pages.
- Tabs: Tonight, Games, Pulse, News, League, Teams, Players, (My team), Trust the model. Keep it at that; new features go inside existing tabs unless they're a new way of following the league.
- Fantasy roster lives in `site/config.json` (player IDs + slots). Update it when Tolar makes moves.
- My team is private: `build.py` writes it only as `data/fantasy.lock.json` (AES-GCM, PBKDF2 key from the `FANTASY_PASSWORD` secret) and writes nothing without the secret. Never publish fantasy data in the clear. The deploy stages only index.html, *.js, style.css and data/ (no config.json or Python).
- Rink markings in the shot maps are neutral on purpose: red means goal and nothing else.
- Season constants `CUR`/`PREV` live in `analysis/models.py`; bump them (and backfill) when a new season starts.
- Game catalogue: `site/games.py` (per-game JSON: shots, xG flow, deserved-to-win via exact Poisson-binomial, goals with NHL clip links, stars, player lines, goalie GSAx) and `site/previews.py` (same-day previews, projected lines from last game's shared ice time). Front end in `site/games.js`.
- `scripts/fetch.py` also saves each game's gamecenter `landing` and `right-rail` (current season only) and re-checks for 3 days until highlight clips post.
- `scripts/write_stories.py` writes recaps/previews with the Claude API (secret ANTHROPIC_API_KEY, model claude-sonnet-5-5), cached in `data/stories/` so each game is written once. House style: no em dashes, facts from the JSON only.
- NHL goal-tracking replays (wsr.nhle.com sprites) are restricted to NHL.com; do not spoof headers to fetch them. Link to official NHL.com clips; never download or rehost video. The one embed allowed is the NHL's own YouTube uploads through YouTube's player (see News rules).
- Every game page also gets `notes` (data-only sentences: the period one team owned, best chance that didn't go in, least likely goal, comebacks, goalie swings, power plays) plus a period box and penalties, so it reads well before or without a recap. Previews get the same (`preview_notes` in `site/previews.py`).
- `site/build.py` also writes `data/team_profiles.json` (special teams, penalties, 5v5 share per team) and `data/players/<id>.json` (per-game log for each skater, plus EDGE extras, `story`, the A3Z card as `a3z` and his goals and assists as `reel`).
- Stories: Sonnet 5.5 thinks by default and thinking counts against max_tokens. `write_stories.py` sends `thinking={"type": "between_tools"}` with max_tokens 2000, rejects anything that stops early or doesn't end on a full sentence, and treats a cut-off cached story as missing. Fact sheets label every team number by abbreviation and precompute records and counts (v1 swapped [away, home] stats and miscounted lists); bump `STORY_VERSION` to rewrite every cached story, and the build hides stories older than the current version. Cached previews store the projected goalie ids; when the starters change (the 2:37 PM build often has ESPN's confirmed ones) the build hides the old preview and `write_stories.py` rewrites it.
- Our own goal replays: `goal_sequences()` in `site/games.py` stores each goal's lead-up (events since the last faceoff, capped at the final 60 s) plus who was on the ice from shifts; `site/replay.js` animates it (puck between recorded moments, gaps over 6 s fast-forwarded, frame stepping, 0.25x-2x). Passes are not in the public data, so never draw or describe them. `#goals/<date>` lists every goal from one night.
- Live layer (`site/live.js`), all from ESPN's public site API (CORS open; the NHL API isn't): scoreboard every 30 s (strip, Tonight cards, probable goalies auto-applied unless the user picked one), game summary every 60 s on `#live/<nhl game id>` (shot map, xG flow, deserved-to-win, rated by `analysis/xg_live.py`, exported to `data/xg_live.json`), injuries (flags on odds, previews, My team; Out/IR sink to the bottom), headlines on `#news` and team pages. ESPN coordinates equal the NHL's; ESPN credits blocked shots to the blocking team; ESPN team ids repeat across leagues, so check the uid for `~l:90~`.
- ESPN's edge returns 403 to headless Chromium; in Playwright tests, intercept ESPN requests and fulfil them with curl.
- `scripts/write_news.py` + `.github/workflows/news.yml` (hourly, own concurrency group): `scripts/media.py` gathers ESPN plus the RSS feeds in `scripts/news_sources.json` (news outlets, Substack newsletters, podcasts, YouTube channels), tags teams and tracked players (full names only), and groups reports of one story (same player or two-word name plus same transaction family, or similar wording, within 36 h). Claude takes stories that involve players we track or 1-2 teams, from headlines and teasers plus our numbers, no web search (~1 cent each, 8 per run max); takes are reused by article id and pruned after 21 days. Everything goes to `data/news/index.json` (stories, reads, listen, watch, highlights), read by the site from raw.githubusercontent.com (no redeploy); `data/news/cache/` keeps the last good YouTube lists and the game-to-highlight map.
- News rules: show headlines, teasers and links only; never republish or rewrite full article text, and never send paywalled or full text to Claude. Podcasts play from each show's public RSS enclosure. YouTube: RSS feed first, then the Data API (`YOUTUBE_API_KEY`, optional), then cache; never scrape YouTube pages. Videos play only through YouTube's own embed (youtube-nocookie), loaded on tap; NHL highlight videos are embedded that way on game pages (the NHL's own uploads through YouTube's player), never downloaded or rehosted. The Watch list skips Shorts, sponsor reads and the NHL channel (its videos only feed game pages).
- Tonight's lineups (`expected_lineups()` in `site/build.py`): price everyone who dressed in any of the team's last 3 games and is still on ESPN's roster, minus ESPN's Out/IR/suspended list, plus players coming back (Out/IR in the last 21 days and since cleared, day-to-day without a recent game, or just traded in). Props carry `lineup` ("last", "recent", "returning") and the site tags non-regulars; prices are conditional on dressing. Team strength uses last game's lineup minus the injured plus the returning. Goalies: ESPN's probable starter when posted (`*_goalie_src: "espn"`), else most starts in the last five, skipping injured goalies. Without ESPN it falls back to last game's lineup.
- `scripts/espn.py` (stdlib): ESPN injuries, probables, rosters, and `data/injuries/history.json` (what makes "coming back" detectable). The hourly news job owns and commits the history; the nightly build only reads/updates it locally (two writers would conflict on rebase). ESPN's per-player injury flags on roster pages lag, so they aren't used.
- Pre-game archive: `archive_tonight()` writes `data/archive/<season>/<date>.json` on every build, rewriting each game until its puck drop and then freezing it (a game first seen after puck drop is kept with `pregame: false` and not graded). The nightly site job commits it. `analysis/track.py` grades the frozen prices against results (goal/2+ log loss vs the naive baseline, lineup and goalie hit rates, totals) into `model.json` → "Live record" on the Trust page. This, not the backtest, is the real test: the backtest was handed actual lineups and starters.
- Market lines: `fetch_market()` in `site/build.py` reads the DraftKings lines the NHL publishes free at `api-web.nhle.com/v1/partner-game/US/now` (moneyline, total, puck line; live only, no props, no history). After puck drop the feed carries in-game odds (a 3-0 lead read as -15000), so games already started skip the feed and keep the pre-game line the archive froze, or show none. Each game gets `market` (vig removed; `implied_total` is the mean that reproduces the over price) next to the model's own numbers; Tonight cards and previews show both, the archive keeps them, and `track.py` grades model vs market (totals MAE, over/under log loss, winner Brier). Game-level market data in the public archive is fine; wager tracking and paid odds still belong in a private repo.
- Narrative layer (`site/narrative.py`, front end `site/pulse.js`): `season_pulse()` builds form (last 10 games vs the model's expected points/goals at the player's actual TOI, z-scored; verdicts earned/partly/bounces for hot and due/real/mixed for cold, judged on one named process measure: own ixG or on-ice xGF vs his rate), streaks and droughts (a drought is "due" only if the chances held up), breakouts (age <= 25, role up: TOI +1.5 or PP share +0.15, and chances or pace up, score >= 3), regression flags (G - ixG >= 2.5 "due to cool", ixG - G >= 2 "due", on-ice luck), NHL-rule rookies, milestones (career before this season from bios plus our season totals), goalie form (last 5 starts GSAx, decisions and shots from raw boxscores) and team streaks/luck. `game_stories()` writes each game's storylines with `priced` and an `effect` sentence (back-to-backs, injuries, goalies and special teams are priced; streaks, revenge games, hometowns, milestones aren't) plus three players to watch per team. Outputs: `data/pulse.json`, `storylines`/`watch` in previews, `headlines` in tonight.json, `story` in `players/<id>.json`, `story` in team_profiles.json, `form`/`fv` on skaters and props. Thresholds are deliberately strict early in the season; every text names its sample. Hometown matching uses the `METRO` town lists; add towns there.
- All Three Zones (Corey Sznajder's hand tracking, paid): raw files and processed tables live ONLY in the private repo Tolar-dio/nhl-private (`a3z/raw/<season>/`, `a3z/tables/<season>/{shots,entries,exits,player_games}.csv.gz`, built by its `scripts/a3z_tables.py`; daily refresh workflow there needs the `A3Z_2026_27_URL` secret). The nightly site job checks out `a3z/tables` into `private/` (gitignored) with the read-only `PRIVATE_DATA_TOKEN` secret. Never commit those files or print their rows here (this repo and its logs are public); the site shows only derived, credited numbers. `analysis/a3z.py`: `cards()` builds the per-player card (5v5 per-60 rates and shares with percentiles among his position, min 60 tracked minutes, 150 to be ranked); `profiles()` adds pass and play mixes (`PASS_CAT`: royal road, behind the net, low to high, other O-zone, neutral/D-zone, no pass; `PLAY_CAT`: rush, forecheck, cycle) with position averages, `watch_notes()` (deterministic "what to watch for" sentences with league context from tracked shots: royal-road shots go in about 25% vs 6% unassisted, carry-ins lead to a chance 25% vs dump-ins 8%), and the league table `site/data/a3z_league.json` (every tracked skater's rates, for the style map). High-danger passes follow Corey's Player List formula: `orr*` plus `oell/oelc/oelr` (behind the net); `oelp*`/`op*` are low-to-high and the bare `s` code is not a slot pass. Front end: `site/plays.js` ("How he plays": notes, a style map of every forward or D with 150+ tracked minutes, three map choices per position, gray dots linking to each player, his dot in blue; percentile bars; mix bars with a tick for a typical player; glossary; credit) and "Highlights" (his goals and assists from `reel` in `players/<id>.json`, each with our Replay and the NHL clip, plus the latest NHL YouTube highlight from the news index). Player header: NHL headshot `assets.nhle.com/mugs/nhl/<season>/<team>/<id>.png` (hotlinked, never rehosted; falls back to the jersey number) and the bio from `story`. No action photos: game photography is licensed (Getty/NHL), so footage stays official clips and YouTube embeds. `current_z()` feeds `models.A3Z_Z`, a points-only nudge `lam_p x exp(0.08 chance-assist z + 0.08 chance z + 0.04 carry-in z)` renormalised to the league level. Tests (Oct 2026): `python analysis/a3z.py xg` (A3Z passing on top of MoneyPuck xG, 25k tracked shots, out of fold: AUC 0.769 to 0.809, log loss 0.2377 to 0.2240; royal-road passes, screens and odd-man rushes carry it, but trackers may see more of the build-up on goals, so treat the size with caution); `python analysis/a3z.py props` (walk-forward on the 2025-26 backtest with 2024-25 tracking as the prior: goal prices no gain; points gain front-loaded, Dec-Feb). The nudge was fit on 2025-26 (1+ -0.0010, 2+ -0.0005, 3+ -0.0001 log loss) and held on 2026-27 (2,943 player-games: -0.0017, -0.0009, -0.0003). A3Z files name games by number, but the numbers are often wrong; the builder matches games by teams and date. Deep dives get the card as `hand_tracking` (credit it by name).
- Player bios (`fetch_bios()` in `fetch_external.py`): NHL player landing for everyone on a roster, refreshed every 45 days (`BIO_MAX` caps a night), saved to `data/external/bios/<season>/bios.json.gz`: birth date and place, size, draft, NHL teams by season, and `career_prev` (career regular-season totals minus this season, so it doesn't go stale). ESPN injury entries now carry `kind` (body part) for the storylines.
- NHL EDGE extras (`fetch_external.py`): a rotation refreshes each player every 5 days, at most 260 a night, from skater-detail, skater-shot-location-detail, skater-shot-speed-detail, skater-skating-distance-detail and goalie-detail. Tables in `data/external/edge/<season>/`: `skaters.csv.gz`, `shot_areas.csv.gz` (17 areas), `hard_shots.csv.gz` (10 hardest per player with game and clock), `distance_games.csv.gz` (a running per-game log built from the last-10 lists), `goalie_areas.csv.gz` (save % by area; `all:high` is high-danger). The site shows shot areas and hardest shots on player pages, miles and top shot speed in the game log, the speed in the shot-map panel when a shot is one of the player's 10 hardest, and high-danger save % on preview goalie cards. Per-area percentiles from EDGE are unreliable at small samples, so they aren't shown. `EDGE_ONLY=<ids>` refreshes specific players when debugging.
- Props carry `p_goal`, `p_1pt`, `p_2pts`, `p_3pts` (Tonight has a market switch; odds typed per market). Back-to-backs (`*_b2b`) are tagged on Tonight and previews.
- Shot arrays. Game files: `[x, y, xg_milli, goal, home, game second, shooter, strength, type code, distance ft, goalie, event id, flags (1 rebound, 2 rush, 4 on net), seconds into shift or -1]` (`site/games.py`; type codes in `SHOT_CODE`), plus 14: shot speed mph when EDGE lists it, else 0. `shots.json`: `[x, y, xg_milli, goal, game number (id % 10000), game second, type code, mph or 0]`; team maps append the shooter id at index 8. The front end (`shotMap()` in `site/app.js`) picks the nearest dot to a tap, rings it, and shows details in a panel under the map (no floating labels on touch); live pages keep the pick across refreshes. Per-shot speed is only public for each player's 10 hardest shots (EDGE).
- News page: five tabs (Top stories, Moves, Features, Listen, Watch) with counts (hidden on phones), a sticky tab bar, paging, `#news/<tab>` deep links (`#news/reads` maps to Features); all panels stay in the DOM so audio keeps playing across tabs, and the Moves kind filter redraws only its own list. `#news/<story id>` is still a take page.
- Moves (`scripts/moves.py`, run by the hourly news job): ESPN's transactions feed (`site.api.espn.com/.../nhl/transactions`, about 1,200 a year, including coach and GM hirings and firings), last 30 days, each entry filed under its biggest move (staff > trade > signing > waivers > suspension > injury > up > down), player names pulled from position tags and matched to our ids, the two sides of a trade folded into one. ESPN's text is shown as written and credited. Claude writes a 50-110 word note on trades and staff changes only (`MOVES_MAX`, cached in `data/news/moves/`, pruned after 45 days). Goes into `data/news/index.json` as `moves`.
- Features (`scripts/write_features.py`, nightly after the stories): Ice Sheet's own writing, Claude from our numbers only, no reporting or quotes, bylined "Written by Claude from Ice Sheet's numbers". One scheduled piece a night: the weekly "Around the league" column when the last is 7+ days old (facts: `site/data/week.json` from `week_digest()` in narrative.py, plus the week's notable moves), else a player deep dive (subject from the Pulse lists, rotating weekly, no repeat within 90 days), else a team deep dive (highest Pulse team weight, no repeat within 45 days). `data/features/requests.json` jumps the queue (`{"kind": "player", "name": ...}`, `{"kind": "team", "team": "COL"}`, `{"kind": "column"}`; two a night, removed once written). Saved to `data/features/<season>/<slug>.json` (committed), published every run to `site/data/features.json` and `site/data/features/<slug>.json`; `#feature/<slug>` renders one, and player and team pages link their latest deep dive. Output format: `TITLE:` and `DEK:` lines, then the body with `## ` subheads.
